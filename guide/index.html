The Anatomy of a Good Prompt

Every effective AI prompt has four components:

  1. Role: Who the AI is acting as — this sets expertise and tone.
  2. Task: What specifically the AI must do — use clear action verbs.
  3. Context: Background information the AI needs to give a good answer.
  4. Format: How the output should be structured — length, style, structure.
You are an experienced financial planner in India. Write a 5-step plan for someone aged 30, earning ₹10 lakh/year, who wants to build ₹1 crore by age 50. Format: numbered list, each step under 50 words, use simple English.

Prompt Engineering Techniques

  • Role prompting: "You are a [expert]. You have expertise in [domain]." Sets voice and accuracy.
  • Chain of thought: "Think step by step before giving your answer." Improves reasoning quality.
  • Few-shot examples: Show 2–3 examples of input/output before your actual query.
  • Constraints: "In exactly 100 words", "Only cite Indian law", "No jargon". Narrows output.
  • Ask for alternatives: "Give me 3 versions of this for different tones." Increases options.

Common Prompt Mistakes to Avoid

  • Being vague: "Tell me about tax" vs "Explain Section 80C deductions for a salaried employee in FY 2024-25."
  • No format instruction: always specify length and structure.
  • Assuming context: the AI doesn't know your situation unless you describe it.
  • Not iterating: if the first answer is mediocre, refine the prompt.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is prompt engineering?

Prompt engineering is designing and refining AI inputs to get the best output. A good prompt includes clear role, specific task, relevant context, and format instructions. The difference between a good and bad prompt is often the difference between a generic and a highly useful response.

What makes a good AI prompt?

Role + Task + Context + Format. Be specific, avoid vague instructions, state your audience, and iterate. If the output isn't right, refine the prompt — add more context or narrow the scope.
